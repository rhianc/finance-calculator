Monthly compounding (default)
$$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n} - 1}$$ Where:
$P$ = loan amount
$r$ = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)
$n$ = number of months (years × 12)
Quick derivation & continuous compounding
Derivation of the monthly formula
Start with the balance after one month. After the payment $M$ is applied:
$$B_1 = P(1+r) - M$$ After two months:
$$B_2 = B_1(1+r) - M = P(1+r)^2 - M(1+r) - M$$ After $n$ months the remaining balance is:
$$B_n = P(1+r)^n - M\frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}$$ At the end of the loan, $B_n = 0$. Solving for $M$ gives:
$$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}$$
Continuous compounding & the definition of $e$
The number $e$ is defined as the limit of compound interest as the number of compounding periods goes to infinity:
$$e = \lim_{m \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{m}\right)^m$$ More generally, continuous growth at annual rate $R$ is:
$$\lim_{m \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{R}{m}\right)^{mt} = e^{Rt}$$ The continuous-compounding monthly payment that amortizes a loan is:
$$M = P \times \frac{R e^{Rt}}{e^{Rt} - 1}$$ (Here $R$ is the continuous annual rate and $t$ is the term in years.) Most car and home loans use the discrete monthly version instead.
