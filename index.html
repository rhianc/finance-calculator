Derivation
Monthly principal & interest
$$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n} - 1}$$ Where $P$ = loan amount, $r$ = monthly rate (annual / 12), $n$ = months.

Remaining balance after $n$ months is the principal grown forward minus the future value of the payment annuity: $$B_n = P(1+r)^n - M \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (1+r)^k$$ The sum is geometric: $$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (1+r)^k = \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}$$ so $$B_n = P(1+r)^n - M \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}.$$ Set $B_n = 0$ and solve for $M$: $$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}.$$ Continuous limit ($m \to \infty$ periods): $$e = \lim_{m\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)^m, \quad M = P \times \frac{R e^{Rt}}{e^{Rt}-1}.$$

Tax benefit explainer

Homeowners can itemize deductions for mortgage interest and property taxes (subject to limits). The estimated tax savings = (monthly interest + monthly property tax) × marginal tax rate.

Those savings are assumed to be invested at the stock return rate and added to the house net worth line. This is a simplified model — real benefits depend on whether you itemize, the SALT cap ($10k federal), loan limits, and your full tax situation.

Default 32% is a rough combined federal + state marginal rate for higher earners. Adjust the knob to match your situation.
