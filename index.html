Monthly principal & interest
$$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n} - 1}$$ Where $P$ = loan amount, $r$ = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12), $n$ = months.
Step-by-step derivation
Start with principal $P$. After one month the balance is:
$$B_1 = P(1+r) - M$$ After two months:
$$B_2 = B_1(1+r) - M = P(1+r)^2 - M(1+r) - M$$ After $n$ months the remaining balance is the geometric series:
$$B_n = P(1+r)^n - M \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}$$ At payoff, $B_n = 0$. Solve for the payment $M$:
$$0 = P(1+r)^n - M \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}$$ $$M \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r} = P(1+r)^n$$ $$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}$$
Continuous compounding (limit as periods → ∞)
The constant $e$ is defined by
$$e = \lim_{m \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{m}\right)^m$$ so continuous growth at annual rate $R$ over time $t$ is $e^{Rt}$. The continuous monthly payment that amortizes the loan is
$$M = P \times \frac{R e^{Rt}}{e^{Rt} - 1}$$ (Most consumer home and car loans use the discrete monthly formula above.)
