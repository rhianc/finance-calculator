Derivation
Monthly principal & interest
$$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n} - 1}$$ Where $P$ = loan amount, $r$ = monthly rate (annual / 12), $n$ = months.

Remaining balance after $n$ months is the principal grown forward minus the future value of the payment annuity: $$B_n = P(1+r)^n - M \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (1+r)^k$$ The sum is geometric: $$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (1+r)^k = \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}$$ so $$B_n = P(1+r)^n - M \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}.$$ Set $B_n = 0$ and solve for $M$: $$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}.$$ Continuous limit ($m \to \infty$ periods): $$e = \lim_{m\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)^m, \quad M = P \times \frac{R e^{Rt}}{e^{Rt}-1}.$$

How the net worth comparison works

House path: Home value grows monthly at the home appreciation rate. Loan balance declines as principal is paid. Equity = home value − remaining balance. Each month the tax savings from deductible mortgage interest plus property tax (capped at the $10k annual SALT limit) are invested at the stock return rate and added to the house side. The result is discounted by inflation to today’s dollars.

Rent + stocks path: The down payment starts invested in stocks. Each month the difference between total ownership cost and rent is added to (or withdrawn from) the stock balance. Total ownership cost = principal & interest + property tax + insurance + maintenance + HOA + PMI (if down payment < 20%). The stock balance compounds at the stock return rate and is also discounted by inflation to today’s dollars.

Both lines are in today’s dollars. The house line includes equity plus the invested tax savings; the rent line is the opportunity cost of the down payment plus the monthly cash-flow difference, both growing at the stock return.

Taxes & assumptions

Tax benefit (modeled): Homeowners can itemize deductions for mortgage interest and property taxes (subject to limits). The estimated tax savings = (monthly interest + monthly property tax) × marginal tax rate. Those savings are assumed to be invested at the stock return rate and added to the house net worth line. Default 32% is a rough combined federal + state marginal rate for higher earners. Real benefits depend on whether you itemize, the SALT cap ($10k federal), loan limits, and your full tax situation.

Selling early: The $250k / $500k primary-residence capital-gains exclusion generally requires you to have owned and lived in the home for at least 2 of the 5 years before the sale. Selling sooner can forfeit or reduce the exclusion. Gains on property held ≤1 year are short-term (taxed as ordinary income); longer holds get long-term rates. Selling also incurs closing costs, realtor commissions (~5–6%), and any transfer taxes.

Other ownership taxes: Ongoing property taxes (modeled), possible capital-gains tax on appreciation when you sell (after the exclusion), and in some places transfer or recording taxes at purchase or sale. This model does not include capital-gains tax, depreciation recapture (more relevant for rentals), or state-specific transfer taxes. Not tax advice — actual results depend on your full situation, filing status, and current law.
