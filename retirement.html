How the lines are counted

Once a month. Savings go in at the end of a working month. In retirement, spending comes out at the start of the month, then what's left grows. Social Security starts at the later of the claim age and retirement, and it is already in today's dollars.

Real return = (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1.

The monthly rate is that real return, compounded. A contribution that rises with inflation is a flat amount in today's dollars, so the savings input is added as-is. Same for spending and Social Security.

The burn rate is a search: the largest spending level, to the nearest $100, whose balance never hits zero before the last month. Your slider is a separate plan drawn on the chart. If a line reaches the floor, that plan has run out, and the age is the first month it could not pay.

Left out: tax on withdrawals, fees, a bad sequence of years, and any pension besides the Social Security number. Not a quote, and not tax or investment advice.
